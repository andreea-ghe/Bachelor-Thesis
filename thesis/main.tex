\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Reconstructing a complete 3D object from its fractured pieces is a long-standing problem with applications in archaeology, paleontology, medicine, and forensics. Given point clouds of the broken fragments, the task is to predict the rigid transformation mapping each piece to its original position---a 6-Degree-of-Freedom pose estimation problem. This thesis investigates whether targeted modifications to the Jigsaw pipeline, the first end-to-end learning-based framework for multi-piece fracture assembly, can further improve reconstruction quality, and what such experiments reveal about the relative importance of architecture, data, and optimization.

Three extensions are proposed and evaluated: (1) a geometric pair attention bias adapted from the GPAT architecture and integrated into Jigsaw's cross-attention layers; (2) a gated double attention module that adds a second round of self- and cross-attention through zero-initialized learned gates; and (3) an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) refinement step inserted between RANSAC-based pose estimation and global synchronization. All experiments are conducted on the everyday and artifact subsets of Breaking Bad, with models finetuned on both two-piece and multi-piece (2--4) fracture patterns.

The proposed modifications yield targeted improvements: gated double attention provides +2--5 percentage points under data scarcity and consistent cross-domain gains on the artifact subset, while ICP refinement adds approximately 1 percentage point across all configurations as a reliable, non-learned enhancement. A data scaling analysis comparing the full dataset against a reduced subset ($\sim$63\%) reveals that data quantity has a large compounding effect, with the full dataset yielding over 10 additional percentage points. The pair attention experiment shows that geometric spatial descriptors depend on their surrounding architectural context to be effective. An interactive web application accompanies the thesis, allowing users to run inference across nine model variants and visually compare reconstruction quality in 3D.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Reconstructing a complete 3D object from its fractured pieces is a long-standing problem with applications in archaeology, paleontology, medicine, and forensics. Given a set of point clouds representing the broken fragments, the task is to predict the rigid transformation that maps each piece back to its original position---a problem known as 6-Degree-of-Freedom pose estimation. Recent learning-based frameworks, most notably Jigsaw, have demonstrated that jointly learning surface segmentation, point correspondence, and global alignment significantly outperforms classical geometry-based methods on the Breaking Bad benchmark.

This thesis investigates whether targeted, modular modifications to the Jigsaw pipeline can further improve assembly quality, and what such experiments reveal about the relative importance of architecture, data, and optimization.

Three extensions are proposed and evaluated. First, we adapt the geometric pair attention mechanism from the GPAT architecture and integrate it as a spatial bias into Jigsaw's cross-attention layers. Second, we extend the feature extractor with a gated double attention module---a second round of self-attention and cross-attention controlled by zero-initialized ReZero gates---designed to deepen feature refinement without destabilizing the pretrained model. Third, we insert an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) refinement step between RANSAC-based pairwise pose estimation and Shonan averaging, exploiting dense fracture surface geometry to tighten alignments. All experiments are conducted on the everyday and artifact subsets of Breaking Bad, with models finetuned on both two-piece and multi-piece (2--4) fracture patterns across two versions of the dataset: an incomplete version with 37\% of the training data silently missing, and the restored complete version.

The central finding is that data quantity dominates architectural changes. During the experimental campaign, we discovered that 37\% of the training data had been silently lost due to a preprocessing issue. Restoring it improved part accuracy from 75.9\% to 86.4\% on everyday objects---an increase of over 10 percentage points---far exceeding any architectural modification. The gated double attention module provided a measurable benefit of 2--5 percentage points when data was limited, acting as an implicit regularizer, but became redundant once the full dataset was available. Analysis of the learned gate values revealed that the model actively uses the second cross-attention layer in a subtractive manner while leaving the second self-attention layer essentially dormant. The pair attention experiment showed that geometric spatial descriptors depend on their surrounding architectural context to be effective, providing empirical evidence for the conditions under which this mechanism is beneficial. ICP refinement yielded a consistent improvement of approximately 1 percentage point across all configurations, providing a reliable, parameter-free enhancement that is orthogonal to the attention-based extensions. An interactive web application built with Django, FastAPI, and Three.js accompanies the thesis, allowing users to run inference across six model variants and visually compare reconstruction quality in 3D.

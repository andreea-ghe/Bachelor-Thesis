{# Slide 5: Future research directions #}

Future Directions

Scale to 2–20 pieces

The current evaluation covers 2–4 pieces. Testing pair geometric bias on richer combinatorial diversity may reveal benefits invisible in the small-piece regime.

Loss schedule search

Experiments showed high sensitivity to when matching and rigidity losses activate. Systematic scheduling or curriculum learning could unlock further gains.

Synthetic → real transfer

Breaking Bad fractures lack erosion, wear, and missing parts found in real archaeological or medical data. Bridging this domain gap is an open challenge.

Missing fragments

Current methods assume all pieces are present. Handling partial reconstructions where fragments are lost or unrecoverable remains unsolved.

The artifact split already serves as a cross-domain benchmark (trained on everyday objects, evaluated on archaeological-style artifacts), pointing toward broader transfer learning paradigms.
